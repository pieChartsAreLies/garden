There's value in creating anything. I'm not talking about the creation of digital items, written word, or anything else that's intellectual in nature, I'm talking about physically using your hands to create things. 

>[!Quote]
>"Making things by hand is an act of defiance and a call to slow down and think about what we're doing." 
>-Deiter F. Uchtdorf

>[!Quote]
>"He who works with his hands is a laborer. He who works with his hands and his head is a craftsman. He who works with his hands and his head and his heart is an artist." 
>-Saint Francis of Assisi

In my life, some of the most rewarding and personally satisfying things that I have done have involved the creation of a physical object. Something tangible and real. Something that provides value to my life and utility. I'm not much of an artist, though sometimes I wish I were more of one, nor am I a skilled craftsman, but I have experienced the joy of creation through [[Pottery Studio Build]], [[wood working]], [[knitting]], [[photography]], and [[home maintenance]] projects.

There's something satisfying and fulfilling about sitting on the couch in the morning, drinking a cup of coffee in a cup that you made, wrapped in a knit blanket that you knit, with your feet up on a coffee table that you made, looking over the photos on the wall that you took and framed. 

When I think about my life, the term  [[renaissance man]] comes to mind. I remember an old friend once commenting that I knew so much about everything and anything. I laughed, as I didn't think it was true, but in reflection, I could see where he was coming from. I value a wide river of knowledge that's deep in some areas, and shallow in others. 

I sometimes look at savants or people that are incredibly skilled in a single task and wish that I could have the dedication and focus to do those things, but my mind, interests, hobbies and desires don't flow like that. I enjoy capturing a modicum of knowledge of a subject, working it and allowing the fleeting passion to slip from my fingers as I move onto the next thing. I think this makes me a well rounded person, and I embrace what it's allowed for me to become. 

I may never be able to play a beautiful piece of music on the piano, or paint a glorious sunrise upon a canvas, but I will be able to teach my children about the world, and dabble in a wide array of areas that bring me joy and fulfilment. And much like this digital garden, I'll be able to re-visit those old ideas, dust off the cobwebs of knowledge, and embrace them in the future.
